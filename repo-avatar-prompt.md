# Repo Avatar Prompt

Image-generation prompt for creating a repository's avatar: an original character drawn entirely from the repository itself. Fill in the bracketed repository context before using it.

## Prompt

Create a circular profile avatar for the GitHub repository described below: an original character in the style of a small handmade designer toy, who embodies what the project is about. Don't base it on GitHub's Octocat, any existing mascot, or a real person's photo. Every choice about the character should come from the repository.

### Repository context

* Name: [repo name]
* README summary: [3–5 sentence summary]
* Tech stack: [main languages/tools]
* Purpose / domain: [what it does and who it's for]
* Activity level: [commits, stars, how active]
* Contributors: [solo, small team, large community; who they are]
* Tone: [playful, experimental, academic, professional, etc.]

### Character concept (most important)

Don't default to a programmer, engineer or hacker. Look past the code and ask what the project is really about: what real-world problem, craft or community it serves. Make the character the person who lives in that world.

* A weather data API becomes a storm chaser or meteorologist.
* A music theory library becomes a conductor or jazz musician.
* A recipe-scaling CLI becomes a pastry chef.
* A map-rendering engine becomes a cartographer or explorer.
* An accessibility toolkit becomes a sign-language interpreter or guide.
* A database optimization tool becomes a librarian or archivist.
* A security scanner becomes a locksmith or detective.

For purely technical projects with no obvious domain, use a metaphor for what the code does: a caching layer is a squirrel storing acorns, a parser is a translator, a scheduler is a train conductor.

### The character

* Body: a small chibi figure with an oversized head, about half its total height, a short rounded body and stubby arms.
* Face: minimal and calm, like a friendly AI companion rather than a loud cartoon mascot. Two small dot or bean eyes, softly blushed cheeks, and a tiny nose or mouth, or none at all.
* Hood or hat: the signature piece. An oversized hood or hat whose shape tells the persona at a glance, such as a storm-cloud hood for a meteorologist, a pointed explorer's hood for a cartographer, or a hood with animal ears for a metaphor character. Add a few small embroidered or appliqué motifs from the project's world, such as stars, leaves, notes or tiny map pins.
* Companion: the character hugs one small companion to its chest, a tiny plush creature or object that stands for what the project does, such as a little raincloud, a book, a star or an acorn. Give it the same simple dot-eye face. This is the main prop. Add at most one other small detail, such as a scarf, badge or pin.
* Avoid laptops, code brackets, terminals and gears unless they truly are the subject.

### Reading the repository

* Colors: 2–3 main colors drawn from the project's domain and mood, kept soft and pastel, with a subtle nod to the tech stack's signature colors, for example in the scarf or the embroidery.
* Energy: very active repos get brighter colors, a slight lean or wave and richer embroidered detail. Small or quiet repos get muted colors, a still pose and a simpler outfit.
* Team size: a solo project gets one personal, handmade-looking detail. A big community gets a subtle team cue, like a badge, a pin or a uniform-colored scarf.
* Material (from the project's tone):
  * Playful, hobby or creative projects: needle-felted wool with fuzzy fibers and visible texture.
  * Experimental, indie or student projects: felt with hand-stitched details and slightly imperfect seams.
  * Retro, gaming or nostalgic projects: felt or clay with pixel-pattern embroidery and a limited palette.
  * Calm, academic or mid-size open-source projects: smooth matte clay with clean, simple shapes.
  * Large-scale or enterprise tech: polished soft-vinyl designer toy with a refined finish.

### Render style

* Photograph it like a real handmade toy: soft diffused studio light, a gentle shadow, a shallow depth of field and a cozy, quiet mood.
* 3D and tactile, never a flat illustration.

### Composition

* Square 1:1 image designed to be cropped to a circle.
* Frame from the chest up, low enough to include the companion hugged at the chest.
* Center the head slightly above the middle. Keep hood tips, ears and accessories at least 10% inset from the edges so nothing gets cut off by the crop.
* Use a solid or softly graded pastel background, such as sky blue, sage or blush, that fills the frame edge to edge and complements the character. A very soft, blurred hint of the project's world, such as a meadow or sky, is fine if it stays simple.
* Use a strong silhouette and clear contrast so it reads at small sizes.

### Text

* Use no text by default. If text is essential, allow at most one short word or the repo's initials on a small badge.
* No logos, watermarks or captions.
